Fair questions

The ones I get asked every time.

Why is it only $400 when the last quote I got was $3,000?

Because there's no office, no account manager and no sales team taking a cut. I'm a computer science student in Brisbane who writes code. The work isn't cheaper — the business behind it is. A five-page site for a local trade is a week of work, not a three-month project, and I'd rather price it honestly and do more of them.

So what does it actually cost me each year?

About $25, and it doesn't come to me. That's your domain name renewing with the registrar — the same bill you'd pay whoever built your site. Hosting is free because I hand-code the pages instead of using a page builder, so there's no software subscription underneath it. If you later want me on call for changes and monitoring that's $25 a month, but it's optional and most people never take it.

Do I actually own it, or are you holding the keys?

You own it. On final payment I hand over the domain, the hosting login and the site files. If you want to move to someone else in six months, you can walk out with everything. Plenty of agencies don't do this. It's worth asking whoever else you're talking to.

I'm flat out. How much of my time does this take?

One twenty-minute call and a handful of photos. I write the copy and send it to you to approve — you're correcting it, not writing it. Most clients spend under an hour in total.

What if I don't like it?

Two rounds of changes are included and we sort it out. The 50% deposit covers the build time; if we can't get it right, you don't pay the balance and I don't put it live. I'd rather lose half a job than have my work on a site the owner isn't happy with.
